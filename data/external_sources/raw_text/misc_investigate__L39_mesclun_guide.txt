Mesclun
CULTIVARS
• Salanova (Red Butter, Red Sweet Crisp, Green Sweet Crisp, Green Incised)
• Ezbruke
• Brentwood
• Avoid Green Oakleaf from the Salanova collection
• The cultivars are mixed in cell flats at time of seeding.

INTENSIVE SPACING
• 4 rows (8” - 20 cm) per bed spaced every 6” (15 cm) on the row

FERTILIZATION
• One cut lettuce will do great in beds that have been previously amended with
compost and is fertilized with a mix of alfalfa meal (2-0-2) and pelleted chicken
manure (5-3-2).

IMPLANTATION
Mesclun is an important cash crop, but it comes with the challenge of not hitting any
shortage of production throughout the season. We seed 3 times the amount of our
weekly needs every 3 weeks and plan multiple cuts per planting. We cultivate the one
cut lettuce on landscape fabric to prevent weeds and to keep the mesclun clean
regardless of the weather. The landscape fabric also keeps the bed clean and weedfree for the following crop.
Our mesclun recipe is 75% cut and come again lettuce (Salanova), 15% baby greens
(mostly mini-kale and red mustard) and 10% frisée.

EQUIPMENT
• Rotary harrow
• Popping tray

ITINERARY
• Remove large debris from the bed, if needed (use flail mower, rake, or manually
remove the large chunks).
• Apply amendments across the bed and pass with the rotary harrow at a depth of
2.5” (6 cm). To make transplanting easier (seeing as the soil is loose after being
harrowed), we only prepare the beds when we are ready to transplant.
• Since the bed is covered with landscape fabric, it does not need to be cleaned up
with another pass of the rake.
• If installing drip tape, set out 4 lines per bed and start the system to check for leaks
or twists. Fix any leaks you notice at that point.
• Lay down landscape fabric and secured with staples each second step (5’ or 1.5 m).
It needs to be really tight.
• Use the popper tray to facilitate extraction and transplant the lettuce seedlings as
fast as possible. 3 people / 100’bed is good ratio for efficient workflow.
• Water the bed thoroughly the first time, keeping the soil moist for the first week
after transplant.

WEEDING
• No weeding required

PLANT PROTECTION
• No action is required.

HARVEST
• Harvesting is done using a VERY sharp knife. Sharpen your blades before each
harvest; this will help reduce potential oxidation (browning/discoloration) of the cut
surface. For the same reason, never use carbon or rusted blades.
• In a kneeling position, slice through the whole lettuce, cutting just above the
growing tip (about 1’’ from the soil). Leaves should not be any bigger than the palm
