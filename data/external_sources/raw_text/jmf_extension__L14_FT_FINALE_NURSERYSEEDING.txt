Seedling production

Seedli g Productio
The production of healthy and uniform transplants is the first step towards
having a successful crop so it must be carried out with great care. To achieve
this, make sure that the containers (trays and pots) are filled evenly, verify the
quality of the soil and provide careful and regular maintenance.
Each week, the propagation calendar is consulted to see which crops and
cultivars need to be started.
In the nursery charts, you will find all the details related to transplant
production for each crop - the number of days in plug cells, the number of
seeds per cell, the type of potting mix, the optimal germination temperature,
the type of container and the number of days before potting up.

Choice of Potting Mix
To produce vigorous seedlings for a diversified farm, you need at least 2 types
of potting mixes. One is for starting most seeds, and the other is for potting up.
●

Low fertility potting mix does not contain compost, has a low salinity
and has a fine texture. These characteristics favor seed germination.

●

High fertility potting mix contains around 15% compost and is geared
towards plant growth.

We use two types of commercial potting mixes. Make sure that all the
ingredients in the substrate you plan to use are permitted by your organic
certifier (some wetting agents are not approved).
Although it is possible to buy the ingredients for the potting mix separately and
make your own recipe, it is much easier to buy the mixes commercially. Buying
commercial potting mix ensures consistent quality and requires less time. It is
also possible to add mycorrhizae to the potting mix (or buy an already
inoculated one) as a strategy to inoculate your fields.

After transplanting, the trays and pots are shaken out and the remaining
potting mix is left in the field. This potting soil is not reused, as it has been
leached of its nutrients. The empty containers are brought back to the nursery
tables. They are rinsed and left on the tables for a few days to solarize. The
clean containers are then stacked for storage. This is much simpler and less
labor intensive than disinfecting them individually with sanitizers.
Do not leave empty containers in the sun for too long as this accelerates their
deterioration. Properly maintained, trays and pots can be used for about 3 4
years.

Filling the Trays
First, add water to the soil. This is done using large plastic tubs on wheels.
Hydrating the peat moss in the potting mix will increase its porosity. For an 85
liter bag, add about 8 liters of water. Mix the water in with a small shovel until
the moisture is evenly distributed. To check if the potting soil contains the right
amount of water, take a handful and squeeze it in your fist. You should see
some water between your fingers, but you should not be able to get it to drip.
For greater efficiency, always mix 2 bags at a time.
●

To fill the trays, place 4 of them in front of you and pour about 4 liters of
potting mix over each of them. Spread the potting soil evenly over the 4
trays, without pressing down, breaking up clumps if necessary.
