Lettuce

CROPS SPECIFICS

→ New Red Fire
→ Adriana
→ Jericho
→ Eruption

→ 3 rows (12” - 30 cm) per bed spaced every 12” (30 cm) on the row

→ Lettuce will do great in beds that were previously amended with compost and fertilized
with a mix of alfalfa meal (2-0-2) and pelleted chicken manure (5-3-2).

Irrigation Type
→ Drip tape (4 per bed) or sprinkler

Irrigation Management
→ Root depth: Shallow (6”-24”)
→ General: Water consistently to avoid bitter taste. Critical to water before head development.

We plant head lettuce every 2 weeks to ensure a constant supply throughout the season.
For every seeding, we plan enough beds for an estimated 3 weeks of supply, using cultivars
that have different DTM.

In our planting program, lettuce is often grouped with crops like green onions, rapini, bok
choy, mini-romaine, mini-fennel, dill, cilantro and mini-kohlrabi — all crops that stay in the
fields +/- 50 days and that have similar crop requirements.

→ Rotary harrow
→ 30” bed prep rake + 3 marking tubes
→ Popping tray

→ Remove tarp and clean out large debris. Use the rake if needed.*

→ Uniformly apply amendments and pass with the rotary harrow at a depth of 2” (5 cm).
To make transplanting easier (seeing as the soil is loose after being harrowed), we only
prepare the beds when we are about to transplant.

→ Using the bed prep rake and 3 tubes, mark the rows both lengthways and sideways.
Bring out the well-watered trays.

→ Once the beds are ready, 1 person will drop the seedlings while another transplants as
fast as possible. Lettuce transplants are very tough and don’t need special care.
2 people/100’ bed is a good ratio for efficient workflow.

→ Install irrigation and water until the soil is deeply moistened.

* If the previous crop is too well established, or if the bed is too messy, mow the crop as low
as possible and harrow at a depth of 2” (5 cm). Add 1’’ (2.5 cm) of compost evenly spread on the
whole width of the bed and transplant into compost following a no-till strategy.

→ Lettuce is cultivated only once using the collinear hoe 14 to 16 days after transplant,
or earlier if the soil starts to crust.

→ No action is required

→ Lettuce is harvested in the fields in a kneeling position.

→ Harvesting is done using a VERY sharp knife. Sharpen your blades before each harvest; this
will help reduce potential oxidation of the cut surface. Do not use carbon or rusted blades.

→ In the pathway, 1 person harvests 2 rows at a time, pushing a bin in front of them. The third
row is then harvested on the walk back up the bed. Remove slugs and dead or damaged
leaves as necessary.

→ Do not overfill bins, you need to be able to stack them without damaging the lettuces.

→ The lettuce heads are washed by quickly dunking them in a sink full of cold water.

→ Use both hands (one lettuce head in each hand) to shake out any excess and place them
on a screen facing down to let them drip-dry for a moment. Make sure the lettuce is in
the water for no more than a minute to avoid getting waterlogged.

→ Pack the lettuces tightly in storage totes, stacking them upside down to let the water
drain while sitting in the cooler.

→ Lettuces are stored in cold room kept at 35 °F (2 °C) and should not stay longer than
3 days before being sold.
