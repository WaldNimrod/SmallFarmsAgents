Eggplant (field)

CROPS SPECIFICS

→ Fairy Tale
→ Nadia
→ Orient Express
→ Orient Charm
→ Hansel

→ 1 rows per bed spaced every 18’’ (45 cm) on the row.

→ Eggplants are heavy feeders that stay in the garden for 100 + day . We front-load the
total amount of fertility requirement at the initial bed prep stage and do not top dressed
them afterwards.

→ Apply lots of compost, +/- 1.5 inch (7 cm) all across the bed along with a dose of nitrogen-rich
organic fertilizers like pelleted chicken manure (5-3-2) and alfalfa meal (2-0-2). Also, add
potassium sulfate (0-0-50) and organic Sul-Po-Mag (0-0-22)

Irrigation Type
→ Drip tape (4 per bed) : Under plastic mulch.
→ No sprinkler to keep foliage dry and thus prevent diseases.

Irrigation Management
→ Root depth: Medium (24”-40”)
→ General: Constant and even moisture from flower through fruit. Eggplants like to dry
down before being watered again.

→ Start transplants 7-8 weeks before transplantation date. Sow seeds in 128 cell flats
in seed-starting soil mix. 1 seed per cell.

→ Place trays on heating mats and maintain floor temperature at 80 °F (27 °C) until emergence.

→ After emergence, maintain the ambient temperature in the nursery around 75°F (24 °C)
during the day and 65°F (18 °C) at night. The soil-mix must be kept moist at all times.

→ Pot-up in 4’’ (10cm) pots 3 weeks after sowing.

→ Plants are ready to transplant when the seedlings are about 7–8 weeks old they should be
10–12” (25-30 cm) tall. Ideally, they will have some flowers that are open or ready to open.

→ Harden off plants by exposing them to outside temperatures and reducing water for 4-5
days before transplanting

Eggplants are planted once a year and like bell peppers and field tomatoes are heat-loving
crops that like to be grown under cover (tunnels, caterpillars).

→ Rotary harrow
→ Broadfork
→ 30” bed prep rake
→ Landscape fabric, staples, drip tape and fittings.
→ 4’ (1.2M) steel bars (cement rods); 1 stake every 3 plants.

→ Before transplanting, make sure minimum soil temperature is above 60°F (16°C ),
20°C is optimal.

→ Pass through with the broadfork.

→ Remove large debris from the bed if needed (use flail mower, rake or manually remove
the large chunks).

→ Apply compost and amendments in the middle of the bed and pass with the rotary harrow
at a depth of 2.5” (6 cm). Since the bed is covered with landscape fabric, it needs not to
be clean up with another pass of the rake.

→ Install 4 lines of drip tape per bed and start the system. Fix leaks at that point, if any.

→ Lay down landscape fabric and secured with staples each second step (5’ or 1.5 m).
It needs to be tight.

→ Transplant bell peppers making sure to extract the seedlings from their pots delicately.
When putting them in the ground, the soil level on seedling should end up being quite
level with the soil.

→ Using a sledgehammer, drive stakes 12’ (30 cm) deep into the ground every 5’ (1.8M).

→ Water the bed thoroughly the first time, keeping the soil moist for the first week after
the transplant.

→ Once the plants have reached +/- 16’’ (40 cm) high, install the first line of bailing twine,
crossing it from the inside-out of each stake. Basket-weaves the plants 1 or 2 more
times as they grow every 12’’ (30cm).

→ Weed through the holes of the landscape fabric 2 or 3 times in the season.

→ We do not prune the eggplants.

→ Young eggplant plants are susceptible to Colorado potato beetles. Weekly scouting
should be schedule as both adults and larvae can quickly defoliate plants. Control by
hand and, in the case of an infestation, use the recommended organic-farming dosage
of Entrust.

→ If plants are flowering but fail to set fruit, the problem is likely (at least in northern
regions) the tarnished plant bug, which particularly appreciates the sap of eggplant a
pepper flowers. In the case of an infestation, use the recommended organic-farming
dosage of Spinosad

→ Harvesting is done twice a week using a pruner and tree planting bags.

→ Eggplants are ready to harvest when the are the size wanted, but they should be firm.

→ Leave about a ¾’’ (2 cm) inch stem on the fruit.

→ Only eggplants that are free of blemishes are harvested. The rest are picked and dis
carded in the pathways.

→ Eggplants are not washed but must be wiped clean if dirty.

→ Eggplants are stored in cold room kept at 47 °F (8 °C) and should not be stored for more
than 4 days before being sold.
