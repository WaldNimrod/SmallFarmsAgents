Green Onion

CROPS SPECIFICS

→ Parade
→ Deep Purple

→ 5 rows (6’’-15 cm), spaced every 6” (15 cm) on the row

→ Green onions will do great in beds that were previously amended with compost and
fertilized with a mix of alfalfa meal (2-0-2) and pelleted chicken manure (5-3-2).

Irrigation Type
→ Drip tape (4 per bed) or sprinkler, although over head irrigation can increase foliar disease

Irrigation Management
→ Root depth: Shallow (6”-24”)
→ General: Steady supply of moisture; if too dry, onions get a strong unpleasant flavor.
Avoid water on leaves to minimize downy mildew

Green onions are one of the most reliable crops. Easy to grow, consistently popular and
they generate a good revenue per bed planted. We plant some every 2 weeks to ensure
a constant supply throughout the season. For every seeding, we plan enough beds for an
estimated 3 weeks of supply.

In our planting program, green onions are grouped with crops like lettuce, rapini, bok choy,
mini-romaine, mini-fennel, dill, cilantro and mini-kohlrabi — all crops that stay in the fields
+/- 50 days and that have similar crop requirements.

→ Once the beds are ready, 1 person will drop the seedlings while 2 others transplant the

combine plugs). 3 people/100’bed is good ratio for efficient workflow.

→ Install irrigation and water until the soil is deeply moistened.

→ Green onions are harvested with their tops and bunched in the fields in a kneeled position.

→ Onions are picked when they sized up between 3-4’’ (10–12 cm). Bottoms should be
white, without any dead leaves — make sure to strip off any leaves that are crushed, or
have signs of rip damage.

→ Make bunches of +/- 9 onions by grabbing 2 clumps and tying them with a rubber band.
Shake off excess soil and make piles of 10 bunches in the aisles.

→ 1 person should harvest at least 60 bunches/hour

→ Green onions are washed using a pressure gun. Upon arriving from the fields, the bins
are transferred to the cart and placed to the left of the sinks.

→ A lot of bunches are placed side by side with the roots facing the operator. The operator
then sprays the soil off with the gun. Turn the bunches over and repeat. Then pile to the right.

→ Once the pile has reached a certain height, put in storage bin and identify the bin.

→ 1 person should package at least 100 bunches per hour.

→ Green onions are stored in cold room kept at 35 °F (2 °C)
