Baby Ginger

Baby ginger is one of the most lucrative crops per unit area. It is highly
dependent on heat and that is why we grow it in our heated greenhouse.
Ginger takes a long time to grow, but it is well worth it as it is a low
maintenance and high yielding crop. The rarity of baby ginger provides a lot of
excitement to customers.

Family

●
Zingiberaceae

Cultivars

●
Use mature organic ginger as your seeds. Those can be bought in any
food store. Any cultivar works.

Crop Planning

We plan for one generation of ginger per season. The crop will take a month
and a half in the nursery and 4 months in the ground to be ready for harvest. In
our climate, the peak growing season of baby ginger is from April to
September.

Ginger can be transplanted into an unheated tunnel or greenhouse, but best
yields are achieved in a heated greenhouse (in temperate climates).

Starting Seeds

●
88 lbs (40 kg) of ginger seeds is needed for one bed. Those need to be
started as seedlings in the nursery before being transplanted to get a
jump start on the long season required to grow ginger.

●
Buy seeds 6 weeks ahead of the transplant date. If seeds are bought
earlier than that, keep them in a cool room at 54 °F (12 °C).

Baby Ginger

●
From seed to transplant, plan for a 4 to 6 week period depending on
light availability and capacity to maintain heat for seedlings. Seeds are
best started in March for a transplant date in April and a first harvest in
August.

●
When ready to seed, cut ginger seeds in pieces of 100g each. Avoid
seeding smaller pieces. Make cuts on the narrower section of ginger.

●
Fill 4 in (10 cm) pots with a mix of 70% high fertility potting mix and 30%
compost. Leave 1 in (2,5 cm) unfilled. Deposit 1 seed per pot. Cover the
seed with a small layer of potting mix and water the seedlings.

●
Keep seedlings moist, but do not over water, since it could induce
rotting of the seed.

●
Germinate seeds at a temperature of 75-82 °F (24 to 28 °C). Use
heating mats in order to get to that heat. In addition, a clear plastic or a
row cover placed on seedlings can also help germination. On sunny
days, take off the clear plastic or row cover, if the temperature under it
goes over 104 to 113 °F (40 to 45 °C). Take off completely the clear
plastic or row cover once seeds are germinated.

Intensive Spacing

1 row per bed, spaced every 4 in (10 cm) on the row.

Fertilization

●
To grow well, ginger needs high doses of nutrients (especially nitrogen
and potassium). Under fertilizing will lead to poor rhizome development.

●
Three fertilizations are needed. One at transplant and one at each of the
two hillings. Use a high nitrogen fertilizer like pelleted chicken manure
(5-3-2) and add one dose of the tomato fertilizing mix (compost (2-1-1),
alfalfa meal (2-0-2), feather meal (11-0-0) Sul-Po-Mag (0-0-22) and
potassium sulfate (0-0-50).

Irrigation

Irrigation Type

●
Drip tape (2 lines per bed), placed on each side of the row.

Irrigation Management

●
Root depth: Shallow (15-24 in [38-61 cm])

●
Likes steady supply of water. Can be on the same irrigation schedule as
tomatoes, eggplants or peppers. Avoid over-watering ginger.

●
General guideline : on a hot and sunny summer day, give 6 water cycles
of 10 minutes each. Soil should be moist, but never soaked.

Implantation

Ginger is ready to be transplanted in the greenhouse as soon as the soil is at
50 °F (10 °C) or more and the seedlings are approximately 11 in (30 cm) high. It
should be around mid-April.

Equipment

●
Broadfork
●
Fertilization
●

●

●
Shovel
●

Itinerary

●
Each bed is prepared just before planting as it makes transplanting
easier (since the soil is very loose after being harrowed).

●
Remove the tarp and use the rake to quickly clean each bed if there is a
lot of large debris.

●
Use a broadfork on each bed.

Baby Ginger

●
Apply fertilization on the bed and pass with the power harrow to a depth
of 2 in (5 cm).

●
Dig an 8 in (20 cm) trench on the center of the bed. Leave a lot of loose
soil around the trench since ginger needs hilling.

●
Using a hose, soak the trench with water and bring the well-irrigated
ginger pots along each bed.

●
Transplant the 4 in (10 cm) pots in the trench all sticked together (4 in
[10 cm] spacing). Make sure to cover all transplants with 1 in (2,5 cm) of
soil, but also keep some loose soil for later hillings.

●
Install 2 drip tapes per bed. One per side and start the irrigation for one
hour after the transplant, then keep the soil moist for the first week
after planting.

Climate Management

●
Ideal temperature range for ginger growth is 60-80 °F (15-26 °C).

●
If problematic to achieve, or in order to cut heating cost, a second
structure of clear plastic can be installed above the growing plants. To
do this, install a clear plastic with holes on two galvanized steel wires
hung high along the bed. Place a heating tube under this additional
layer.

●
It is also a good strategy to place the ginger in the center of the
greenhouse, where the climate is hotter and more humid.

Weeding and Maintenance

●
To avoid weeding, soil around the ginger plants can be recovered with a
white plastic.

●
Since ginger is a very competitive plant, two hand-weeding should be
enough to control weeds (if your soil is not covered).

●
Ginger should be hilled and fertilized twice in the season. Each time, the
idea is to add at least 4 inches of soil on top of the ginger, mixing in the
amendments.

Baby Ginger

●
On the second pass, the plants are already quite big, and the hiling can
be done with a rotary plow to speed up the process.

Plant Protection

●
Before transplant, a preventive application on seedlings of Trichoderma
harzianum (Rootshield) is recommended to avoid fungal diseases like
Pythium. Apply the recommended dosage by the manufacturer. If leaves
are overly yellow or wilting, it could be caused by those diseases.

●
After transplant, there are very few pests that affect ginger. Look out for
spider mites and fungus gnats, although the risk is fairly low.

Harvest

●
Start harvesting your crops four months after the initial transplant in
order to sell baby ginger (pink skin with juicy and tender flesh). At that
stage, the ginger will have developed big rhizomes, but won’t have yet
developed its rough skin (each seed generating a rhizome at least 4
times its size). If rhizomes are too small, a few growing weeks can be
added before harvest.

●
Harvest ginger by first loosening the rhizomes in the soil with a fork.
Then, slowly pull the ginger out of the soil by pulling on the leaves.

●
Since ginger can stay in the soil for many weeks without damage,
harvest each bed progressively. Harvest each week the quantity that
you are able to sell until there is none left. Note that after 10 months in
the soil, ginger is at full maturity. Between month 4 and month 10 it will
grow its beige rough skin.

Conditioning

●
Rinse rhizomes.

●
Cut roots with scissors or cutters.

●
Cut rhizomes to the size needed and rinse again until no more soil is left.

●
Leaves can be sold with the rhizomes or cut off.

1 person should package 44 lbs (20 kg) of ginger per hour.

Storing

●
Harvest the day of your market/delivery or a day before. Store ginger
inside a plastic bag placed in a closed container at a temperature around
36 °F (2 °C).
